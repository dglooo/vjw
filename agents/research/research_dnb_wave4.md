# Research: DnB wave 4 (Neuro Serpent + Amen Shatter), S1 codebase map + visual refs

## TL;DR
- **Neuro Serpent:** no scene draws a lit, opaque 3D surface today. Every 3D native (wireform, orb, vortex, creatures) is additive points/lines with **no depth buffer** (no `DEPTH` anywhere in `web/src`). Reusable parts: `orbitVP`, the HDR target + bloom, the analytic studio `env()` from `own/liquid-chrome.fs`, the wireform screw's analytic tube frame and VS twist, and lasers' instanced-quad pipeline. Tube options: CPU spline + parallel-transport / double-reflection frames → instanced ring mesh (cheap geometry), or an SDF capsule-chain raymarch (per-pixel cost ∝ steps × segments).
- **Amen Shatter:** the Amen break is 4 bars (Winstons 1969, G. C. Coleman, ≈136–138 BPM). Bars 1–2 are a funk beat; in bars 3–4 the beat-4 snare comes an 8th late; bar 4 leaves beat 1 empty and crashes early. The history ring (`scenes/history.ts`, 32 × RGBA8 slots, `past(uv, age)`) is the stutter/repeat store. At 174 BPM a bar is ≈83 frames, longer than the ring's 64-frame span at stride 2.
- **Signals:** kick/snare/hats reach natives only as decaying envelopes (bound inputs). Per-hit times, strengths and drum labels are not in `FrameInput`. ⚠️ Gaps: ghost notes (not detected), 32nd snare rolls (under the 50 ms minGap), no automatic downbeat (bar phase is arbitrary until the user presses the downbeat key), and no reese "growl" timbre feature (only a level above its 4 s average).

### §1.2 Tube / ribbon along a spline (WebGL2)
- **Frames.** Frenet frames flip at inflections and are undefined on straight runs. Parallel transport (Hanson & Ma 1995, [Indiana TR425](http://www.cs.indiana.edu/pub/techreports/TR425.pdf), which is motivated by exactly this Frenet failure) rotates the previous normal by the angle between successive tangents. three.js `Curve.computeFrenetFrames` does exactly this despite its name (it cites TR425), then spreads the closed-curve twist mismatch evenly ([Curve.js L338–437](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/extras/core/Curve.js), MIT). The **double-reflection** RMF (Wang, Jüttler, Zheng, Liu, ACM TOG 27(1), 2008, [doi 10.1145/1330511.1330513](https://doi.org/10.1145/1330511.1330513), [PDF](https://i.cs.hku.hk/GraphicsGroup/publications/pdf/Computation%20of%20rotation%20minimizing%20frames.pdf)) uses two reflections per step and has 4th-order global error. The projection and rotation methods have 2nd-order error.
- **Instanced rings (mesh).** The CPU evaluates the spline at R samples, the frames and a per-ring radius/twist into a small float buffer or texture. One instanced draw builds the R × S vertices in the VS (ring = instance, angle = vertex). Cost (derived): 256 rings × 32 sides = 8 192 vertices / 16 384 triangles, which is negligible vertex load. The fragment cost is the screen coverage × the shading, about once per pixel with a depth test. Upload: R × ~12 floats per frame. A twist/ripple/"tear" displaces vertices along the normal in the VS, as wireform does (`scenes/wireform.ts` L104–105).
- **Swept ribbon (flat strip).** The same frames with 2 vertices per sample, as a TRIANGLE_STRIP. The TD analogue is the Sweep SOP's "twist and roll" (§1.1).
- **SDF capsule-chain raymarch.** The tube is the min over N capsule segments between spline points; `fCapsule(p, a, b, r)` exists in `donor/hg_sdf/hg_sdf.glsl` L325–326 (MIT OR CC-BY-NC-4.0). Per-pixel cost = steps × N segment evaluations. Derived: 64 steps × 32 segments × 1.93 M px (1728×1117) ≈ 4 × 10⁹ capsule evaluations per frame, so it needs fewer segments, a bounding test, or a lower resolution. A twist inside the SDF is not an exact distance and needs smaller steps (⚠️ unverified today: iq's distfunctions page did not load).
- **Self-occlusion.** A coiling tube overlaps itself. A mesh needs a depth attachment (none exists in the repo, see §1.4). A raymarch resolves occlusion per pixel without one.

### §1.3 Cheap chrome (cost notes)
- **Analytic env (have).** `env(r)` in `web/src/fx/own/liquid-chrome.fs` L36–44: a sky gradient over a tilted, sharp horizon, a dark floor, a soft-box stripe and a pow-48 highlight. It is a few ALU ops and **0 texture fetches**. The surface uses `reflect(view, n)`, then `0.6 + 0.4·n.z` darkens steep flanks (L53–54). Own code.
- **Matcap / lit sphere:** one 2D texture fetch indexed by the view-space normal's xy. It is the cheapest image-based look. The lighting is fixed to the view, so it does not change as the camera orbits ([three.js MeshMatcapMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/#api/en/materials/MeshMatcapMaterial)). Texture source: [nidorx/matcaps](https://github.com/nidorx/matcaps) has **no licence**. Its README says the textures were gathered from ZBrush sites and the original authors are unknown.
- **Fresnel:** Schlick's approximation F = F0 + (1 − F0)(1 − cosθ)⁵, a handful of ALU ops ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlick%27s_approximation)). For a metal, F0 is the tint colour, so the grazing rim goes white.
- **Env BRDF without a LUT:** Karis's `EnvBRDFApprox`, an analytic fit based on Lazarov's work that replaces the split-sum 2D LUT fetch ([UE blog 2014-09-10](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/blog/physically-based-shading-on-mobile)).
- **Roughness blur** of an analytic env: widen the env's smoothsteps per roughness, at no extra fetches (derived from `env()`'s structure). A cubemap or prefiltered env needs texture generation; no cubemap code exists in `web/src` (grep `TEXTURE_CUBE_MAP|samplerCube` in `web/src`: 0 hits).
- **HDR + bloom (have):** `renderTarget(hdr=true)` gives RGBA16F when `EXT_color_buffer_float` exists (`web/src/gl/engine.ts` L22, L80–81). `createBloom` is a dual-filter bloom, threshold 0.5 (`web/src/gl/bloom.ts` L1–8, L44). The tone map is `COMPOSITE_FS` (`web/src/scenes/particles.ts` L83). The lasers' mip-chain haze is a fog/haze precedent (`web/src/scenes/lasers.ts` L1–9).

## §2 Amen Shatter

### §2.1 The Amen break and jungle breakbeats
- **Source:** "Amen, Brother", the Winstons (1969 B-side of "Color Him Father"), drummer Gregory C. Coleman. The four-bar break lasts ≈7 s at ≈1:26 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amen_break)). The original tempo is ≈136 BPM ([PedagoNet, via Wikipedia's links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amen_break)) or ≈138 BPM ([Ethan Hein](https://www.ethanhein.com/wp/2025/amen-break-listening-guide/)). At 174 BPM it plays ×1.26–1.28 faster (derived).
- **Kit:** kick, snare, ride (no hi-hat in the bars; one open hat in a later description) and one crash ([lilys.ai transcript notes](https://lilys.ai/notes/160328), [Ethan Hein Substack](https://ethanhein.substack.com/p/building-the-amen-break)).
- **Bar structure** (Wikipedia + Hein, consistent): bars 1–2 are the same funk beat. Bar 3 delays a snare. Bar 4 leaves beat 1 empty, plays a syncopated pattern and crashes early on a weak 8th. In bars 3–4 the beat-4 snare comes an 8th late; Hein calls this "probably the most crucial element". The ride and snare velocities vary on every hit (Hein listening guide).
- **16th-grid positions** (Hein's 13-step recipe mapped onto 16 steps by the fetch tool, so ⚠️ unverified as an exact transcription). Ride on the 8th off-beats. Kicks on 1 and 3-and, doubled on 3-and in bars 1–2 and on 1-and in bar 4. Snare pickups (ghost-like 16ths) just before beat 3, before 3-and and before the next downbeat. The sources disagree on the bar-3 details. Before treating the grid as exact, confirm it against a MIDI file (NI links one: [NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/best-drum-breaks/)) or the recording.
- **Microtiming:** at the 16th level, Coleman's playing spans ≈120–165 BPM-equivalent around a steady pulse (Hein listening guide). A 16th grid quantises it.
- **Modern DnB two-step** (programmed, not the Amen): 168–178 BPM, swing 50–60 %; kick on steps 1 and 11, snare on 5 and 13 (the steps are from a reader comment); quieter, swung 16th ghost notes at the end of the bar; a breakbeat loop layered, then sliced and reprogrammed; a "slinky downward roll" ends 8 bars ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/raw-drum-bass/)). The 1.5-beat kick period (3-and → 1) is already noted in `web/src/audio/sync.ts` L34–36 / L92.
- **How junglists chop it:** sped up, sliced and rearranged, with a focus on the syncopated back half. A common method is several sampler pads, each starting at a different slice (Hein). Wikipedia says the components were re-ordered and pitch- or speed-altered "to mimic ghost notes".
- **Algorithmic chopping (BBCut, Nick Collins; SuperCollider; GPL-2.0 mirror [nhthn/BBCut](https://github.com/nhthn/BBCut), last push 2016-11-12).** These defaults are from an Ableton port's README ([bencodec/BBenCut](https://github.com/bencodec/BBenCut), no licence, push 2026-06-07). BBCutProc11: 8 subdivisions per phrase, repeat chance .2, repeat speed ×2, repeats in the last half of the phrase. ChooseCutProc: roll chance .1, rolls ≤ 2 beats. WarpCutProc1: straight .5, arithmetic (even) .7 vs geometric, accelerate .6, factor .9. ThrashCutProc1: snare offset .125. Collins's 2001 paper ([generativeart.com PDF](https://www.generativeart.com/on/cic/ga2001_PDF/collins.pdf)) models early-90s jungle cutting. ⚠️ Its text did not extract here, so the parameters come from the port only.

### §2.2 VJ references (beat-locked slicing / AV sampling)
- **Coldcut VJamm (≈1997–98):** Coldcut's own AV sampler, built to re-create whole audiovisual pieces live. v1 was "monophonic", one clip at a time ([Hex (VJ group), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hex_(VJ_group)), [XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/?p=114590)). The sources disagree on 1997 vs 1998.
- **Coldcut & Hexstatic "Timber":** Stuart Warren-Hill edited it in early Premiere "like a drum machine with timing markers", and says it is a myth that VJamm made it ([VDMX blog](https://vdmx.vidvox.net/blog/stuart-warren-hill-holotronica)). Technique name: **video scratching**, a video sample manipulated to follow the music's rhythm ([Wikipedia mirror](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Video_scratching)).
- **Resolume:** BPM-synced parameters run in beats, not seconds, and Resync jumps everything to beat 1 of the phrase ([Resolume parameter animation](https://resolume.com/article/62)). No built-in beat-locked slice displacement was found. Users rely on envelopes, pre-rendered clips, or third-party plugins such as Slice Displace / JUMPER ([forum](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=83082), [kpthippo JUMPER](https://kpthippo.gumroad.com/l/Jumper)).
- **Donor glitch shaders (MIT, rewrite by analogy):** `donor/ISF-Files/ISF/Glitch Shifter.fs` (155 lines, random block shift H/V, size), `Time Glitch RGB.fs` (461 lines, 8 persistent frame buffers, a per-block random delay into them, `rand(TIME)`), `Pixel Shifter.fs` (rows shifted by a phase/frequency), `FastMosh.fs` (two persistent key-frame buffers, a fake datamosh), `JPEG Block Corruption.fs`. `donor/gl-transitions-2.0.0/glsl3/`: `WindowSlices.glsl` (gre), `GlitchBands.glsl` (Y. Maltsman), `StripDatamoshGlitch.glsl` (bread; torn strips over 42 bars / 18 slits), `BlockDissolve.glsl`, `GlitchDisplace.glsl` (M. DesLauriers). Every one of them is driven by a random hash of TIME or by a 0..1 `progress`; none is driven by a beat pattern.

### §2.3 Techniques and cost notes
- **Frame budget at 174 BPM, 60 fps (derived):** beat 0.345 s = 20.7 frames; 8th = 10.3; 16th = 5.2; bar = 82.8. A 32nd roll hit is every 43 ms (2.6 frames). ⚠️ the 60 fps display rate is assumed.
- **Per-slice UV offset from a pattern table.** Slices (rows, columns or blocks) index a small table (a uniform array, or a 16×N texture of offsets and ages). The shader does 1 table read + 1 source read per pixel, the cost of one fullscreen copy. Measured precedent: one half-float feedback pass ≈ 1 ms at 1728×1118, and echo 2.55 ms median over a tunnel (`research_td_look.md` L102, citing `plan_effects.md` L448).
- **Block quantisation:** `floor(uv · grid) / grid` gives the slice id; a hash of (id, bar index) holds a pattern for the bar. `native/glitch` already does this per 50 ms seed (`web/src/scenes/glitch.ts` L62–65, L90). That is the random-not-beat precedent spec §4 rules out.
- **Stutter / repeat from a frame history.** A slice reads `past(uv, age)`, where age loops inside a window L frames long (a repeat), or holds a fixed source frame (a freeze). The ring needs depth ≥ L. Ring sizes: 32 slots at stride 1 = 0.53 s = 1.55 beats; at stride 2 (time-machine, slit-scan) = 64 frames = 3.1 beats at 174 BPM. A whole-bar repeat (83 frames) does not fit either (derived).
- **Ring memory at the 1728×1117 stage:** ½-stage slots of 864×559 RGBA8 × 32 = 61.8 MB, under the 64 MiB cap that `ringSize` enforces (`web/src/scenes/history.ts` L12–18; derived).
- **Taps:** `past()` always samples 2 slots and mixes them, even at an integer age (`web/src/scenes/history.ts` L62–66).
- **Re-assemble then shatter:** set the offsets to 0 for one beat, then widen them. The beat boundary comes from `sync.beat`'s floor (§3).

## §3 Signals (what each scene needs; gaps for `audio-dsp`)
- **What a native sees:** `FrameInput` = energy, `onsets` (broadband, this frame), `lastOnset`, `audioNow`, `sync`, time, dt, size (`web/src/frame.ts` L7–17). The drums, bass and arrangement arrive only as bound `Sources` in `v` (`web/src/bind.ts` L26, `evaluate()` L115–127). **No per-drum hit list with times reaches a native.** implication: a pattern-locked slicer reads envelopes, or it needs a new frame field.
- **reese** (DnB preset band 60–250 Hz; `web/src/audio/presets.ts` L19, L31–37). The level is kick-gated: it holds `HOLD` 0.2 s after a kick trips the band's gate (`web/src/audio/features.ts` L19–22, L63–67). The source = max(0, level ÷ its 4 s running mean − 1), so a steady bed reads 0 and twice its average reads 1 (L17, L28–38, L70). It is × sens and capped at 1 (L113). On real DnB masters it rests at 0 and tracks drop > body > break (`_adhoc/real_tracks_2026-11-05.md` item 7). The DnB preset has no Auto-style remap for reese; Dubstep maps reese → wobble (`presets.ts` L36, L44).
- **wobble** (150–1500 Hz) uses the same rise-above-average rule (`features.ts` L71–73). **wobbleDepth** is a 1 s EMA of the wobble's deviation (L74); raw, it reads .9–1.0 in quiet intros (real_tracks item 7). **sub** (30–60 Hz) works like reese (L69).
- **kick** is a 40–150 Hz gate-ratio transient: ratio ≥ 1.35, kick bins ≥ 0.2 of the hop's power, `MIN_GAP` 60 ms (`web/src/audio/drums.ts` L13, L23–31). **snare**: 1–5 kHz flux, DnB floor 40, tilt veto (L18–21, L33–35). **hats**: 7–16 kHz, floor 50. Envelope τ: kick .12 s, snare .12 s, hats .06 s (`features.ts` L14). After the 2026-11-05 tuning, on the user's DnB tracks: snare 1.1–2.3 per bar, kicks +44–169 % over the 0.4–1.2 per bar measured before (`agents/STATE.md` § Key facts), hats 3.7–5.4 per bar at floor 50 (real_tracks item 1).
- **Hit timing:** 512-point FFT every 256 samples, i.e. a 5.3 ms hop at 48 kHz (`web/src/audio/analysis.ts` L1, L11–12). Peak picking needs the ODF ≥ the previous 3 hops (`web/src/audio/onset.ts` L18). A snare waits one extra hop for the kick-crosstalk check (`drums.ts` L87). For a file, hits wait in the queue until audible; for line-in they apply on arrival (`web/src/app.ts` L250, L279–284). An envelope reads its peak on the frame its hit is drained (`features.ts` L107). Line-in display lag is ≈30–60 ms (derived, `research_audio_analysis.md` L8). ⚠️ The end-to-end hit → pixel latency against R6's "within 2 frames" (33 ms at 60 fps) has not been measured.
- **No velocity:** the hit `strength` is dropped (`features.ts` L85). An accent and a ghost note produce the same envelope.
- **Grid:** `sync.beat` is continuous (floor = the beat index, fract = the phase); `bar` = (beat − downbeatOffset) / 4; `confidence` is 0..1 (`web/src/audio/sync.ts` L7–12). A PLL corrects on onsets within ±⅛ beat (L30, L108–128). Bound beat/bar shapes are × confidence (`bind.ts` L22, L117–120). A 16th slot = floor(4 · beat) (derived).
- **build / drop:** `build` 0..1 (the breakdown's tension); `drop` = the confidence of the last drop, decaying with τ 1 s (`web/src/audio/arrange.ts` L22–26). The ≥ 4 kHz `high` energy (risers, rolls) feeds it (L14). After tuning, 7 of 11 DnB drops fire (`STATE.md` § Key facts).
- ⚠️ **Gap: downbeat.** It is set only by the user's key (`app.ts` L158 → `sync.ts` L172–175). At a lock `downbeatOffset` is 0, so `bar`'s phase relative to the music's "1" is arbitrary. The Amen pattern repeats per bar and per 4 bars.
- ⚠️ **Gap: ghost notes.** Not detected at floor 40. At floor 20, 3.4–5.1 snares per bar fire, but "ghosts and hats creep in" (real_tracks item 1), so ghost vs hat is not separated.
- ⚠️ **Gap: snare rolls.** The snare `minGap` is 50 ms (`onset.ts` L14). A 32nd at 174 BPM is 43 ms, so at most every other hit of a 32nd roll can fire (derived); 16ths (86 ms) fit. Before the tuning, builds fired 0–1 snares per bar against 6–17 offline flux peaks (real_tracks item 1). ⚠️ This was not re-measured at floor 40.
- ⚠️ **Gap: growl timbre.** No spectral-centroid, modulation-rate (LFO speed) or detune feature exists. - ⚠️ **Gap: Amen material.** The kick gate was tuned on modern DnB masters. The user's tracks (Kanine, Sub Focus, Andy C) are not Amen-based jungle, and detector rates on a chopped Amen are unknown.
- ⚠️ **Gap: slot position.** No code assigns a hit to its 16th slot within the bar, so "which hit of the pattern" is not available.
