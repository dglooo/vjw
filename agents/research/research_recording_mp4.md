# Research: REC timing (Chrome MP4 drift, MediaRecorder workarounds, WebCodecs path)

## TL;DR
- **The QuickTime drift comes from Chromium's MP4 muxer.** It never gives a fragment's last sample the next fragment's start time; it writes `1000/frame_rate` ms instead, and `frame_rate` falls back to **30** because canvas frames carry none. The code is the same on `main`, M156 and M157. No crbug or CL was found, so no fix has landed or been announced.
- **A second MP4-muxer bug hits 30-min takes.** The muxer counts written bytes in a `uint32` and CHECK-crashes the renderer past **4 GiB** (crbug 574578846). It is fixed on main 2026-10-22, first in **M158** (stable 2026-11-17); Chrome 156/157 still have it. At our ~3.0 MB/s that is **≈24 min** (derived).
- **MediaRecorder side.** Fragments break only at keyframes, so `videoKeyFrameIntervalDuration` (present in M156) shrinks the drift but cannot remove it. At 30 fps the wrong duration is about right anyway.
- **WebCodecs path.** Chrome on Mac encodes H.264 on VideoToolbox and AAC on AudioToolbox; Chrome's AAC encoder stamps its priming as signal. mediabunny (MPL-2.0, very active) writes correct per-fragment durations and an `elst` for negative starts, but 1.62.1 writes no AAC `roll` group, so AVFoundation trims 2112 samples on top (issue #549 open, PR #580 open).
- **Post-fix.** An `ffmpeg -c copy` remux rebuilds the durations from `tfdt`; adding `-itsoffset` on the audio input gives an `elst` plus a roll group (⚠️ composed from docs and source, not run).

## §1 Is this a known Chromium bug?
Short names: **[frag]** = [`media/muxers/mp4_muxer_delegate_fragment.cc`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/muxers/mp4_muxer_delegate_fragment.cc), **[del]** = [`mp4_muxer_delegate.cc`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/muxers/mp4_muxer_delegate.cc), **[box]** = [`mp4_fragment_box_writer.cc`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/muxers/mp4_fragment_box_writer.cc).

**Mechanism (source):**
- **Last sample of each fragment.** `AddDataToRun` appends each sample's own timestamp; nothing appends the next fragment's first timestamp to the previous `trun` [frag]. At flush, `MaybeFlushMoofAndMfraBoxes` calls `AddVideoLastTimestamp(Milliseconds(kMillisecondsTimeScale / video_frame_rate_))` for **every** fragment [del]. Each trun duration is the difference of consecutive timestamps [box].
- **Where the 30 comes from.** `video_frame_rate_` is the first frame's `frame.metadata().frame_rate.value_or(0)` ([muxer.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/muxers/muxer.cc)); 0 becomes `kDefaultVideoFrameRate = 30.0` ([media_recorder_handler.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/mediarecorder/media_recorder_handler.cc)). The timescale is therefore 30 000, and 33.333 ms converts to **999** ticks because `ConvertToTimescale` truncates ([mp4_type_conversion.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/muxers/mp4_type_conversion.cc)). This matches the measured 999.
- **`captureStream(60)` probably doesn't help.** `CanvasVideoCapturerSource` uses its `frame_rate` only in `GetPreferredFormats`; no frame-metadata write was found ([canvas_capture_handler.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/mediacapturefromelement/canvas_capture_handler.cc)). So `captureStream(60)` most likely still yields 30 ⚠️ (not executed).
- **Where fragments break.** Only at a video keyframe: `MaybeForceFragmentFlush` runs on keyframes, at least 100 ms apart ([mp4_muxer.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/muxers/mp4_muxer.cc)). The default keyframe interval is `kDefaultKeyIntervalCount = 100` frames ([key_frame_request_processor.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/mediarecorder/key_frame_request_processor.cc)), matching the measured 100.
- **Audio takes the same path (derived).** `44100/1024u = 43` and `1000u/43 = 23 ms` give **1014** ticks against a real 1024 at timescale 44100 [del]: −10 samples per fragment, ≈ −0.24 s of audio-track duration per 30 min in a duration-summing reader. ⚠️ Not measured.

**Status:**
- **No crbug or chromium-review CL was found** for the duration logic. Web searches came up empty; issues.chromium.org search needs sign-in ⚠️.
- **The logic already exists at** [8fd19c0](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/commit/8fd19c05188e3edea14b73fe622af46186f56b0e) (2024-07-16, "Make quickPlayer/Safari runs MP4 file from Chromium MediaRecorder", bug 348923066), close to the Chrome 126 MP4 launch. [frag] has had no substantive change since; its last change was a 2025-07-09 refactor ([history](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/commits/main/media/muxers/mp4_muxer_delegate_fragment.cc)). Present on the M156 and M157 branches; no public fix landed or scheduled.

**Related bug, fixed:**
- [CL 8623078](https://chromium-review.googlesource.com/c/chromium/src/+/8623078), commit [3f05755](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/commit/3f05755592) (2026-10-22, main@{#1725131}, "Fixed: 574578846"): "counted every byte … in a uint32_t and CHECK-failed once the total passed 4 GiB, crashing the renderer during long MediaRecorder MP4 recordings … WebM recordings are not affected."
- The CHECK in `output_position_tracker.cc` is still on the M156 and M157 branches ([8078](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/refs/branch-heads/8078/media/muxers/output_position_tracker.cc)). #1725131 lies between the M157 (#1717349) and M158 (#1727472) branch points, so M158 is the first release with the fix. A merge back to 156/157 ⚠️ is unverified.
- **Implication:** at 3.0 MB/s (S4.3: 362 MB in 120 s), 4 GiB arrives at ≈ **23.9 min** of an MP4 take on Chrome 156/157 (derived).

## §2 MediaRecorder-level workarounds (current Chrome)
- **Keyframe options.** `videoKeyFrameIntervalDuration` (ms) and `videoKeyFrameIntervalCount` are both in the M156 IDL; passing both throws `NotSupportedError` ([media_recorder.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/mediarecorder/media_recorder.cc)). Intent to Ship: April 2023 ([blink-dev](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/2ydi1kTIlp0/m/5VlmcX7eBQAJ)); the shipping milestone is not stated anywhere ⚠️.
- **Effect on the bug (derived).** One wrong duration per fragment, and a fragment is one GOP, so drift ≈ fragment count × (1/30 − 1/60 s): a 10 s GOP gives ≈ 3.0 s per 30 min, a 60 s GOP ≈ 0.5 s.
  - Chrome's VideoToolbox session forces a keyframe at `MaxKeyFrameInterval` 7200 frames / `MaxKeyFrameIntervalDuration` 240 s ([vt_video_encode_accelerator_mac.mm](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/gpu/mac/vt_video_encode_accelerator_mac.mm)). At 60 fps that caps a GOP at 120 s, ≈ 0.25 s per 30 min.
- **Cost of a long GOP (source + derived).** A fragment is flushed only at the next keyframe, so the renderer holds about GOP × 3 MB/s, and seek points get coarser ⚠️ (not measured).
- **The 30 mode.** 999 ticks against a real ≈ 1000 is −33 µs per fragment (derived): practically no drift.
- **`captureStream(fps)` vs `requestFrame()`.** Neither sets the muxer frame rate, per §1 ⚠️. `captureStream(0)` + `requestFrame()` only controls *when* a frame is captured (render §5).
- **Other mimeTypes.** `avc3`, `hvc1`/`hev1`, `vp9` and `av01` in MP4 all go through the same `Mp4MuxerDelegate`: same duration bug, same 4 GiB crash (source). WebM/MKV use the separate WebM muxer (block timestamps; "not affected" by the 4 GiB bug per the commit above), with audio `opus` or `pcm` only (render §5).
- **WebM in QuickTime.** In an [Apple Dev Forums thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/832486) (Jun 2026), a community answer says Safari has its own WebM demuxer, AVFoundation has none, and MediaExtension (a custom demuxer) is the only route; the Apple engineer calls it a "container file format" issue. That QuickTime Player can't open .webm/.mkv rests on community sources only ⚠️.
  - Apple says Opus decoding "is available … on all Apple platforms". Opus-in-MP4 playing in QuickTime from Sonoma on is a community claim ⚠️ ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255926188)).
- **Bitrate (source).** MediaRecorder's encoder wrapper sets `VariableBitrate(bps, 2×bps)` and latency `Quality`, with no `framerate` ([media_recorder_encoder_wrapper.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/mediarecorder/media_recorder_encoder_wrapper.cc)). The VEA adapter then uses `kDefaultFramerate = 30` ([video_encode_accelerator_adapter.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/video/video_encode_accelerator_adapter.cc)), which reaches VideoToolbox as `ExpectedFrameRate`. Implication: fits the measured 2× at 60 fps and 1× at 30 (hypothesis ⚠️).

## §3 WebCodecs path
- **`VideoEncoder` (H.264).** `no-preference` and `prefer-hardware` build the platform VideoToolbox encoder first; `no-preference` wraps it with an OpenH264 fallback. With no `framerate` given, 30 is used ([webcodecs/video_encoder.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/webcodecs/video_encoder.cc)).
  - VideoToolbox session: `RealTime` follows low-delay mode, `AllowFrameReordering` is false (no B-frames), and low-latency rate control is used only for hardware + `realtime` ([vt mm](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/gpu/mac/vt_video_encode_accelerator_mac.mm)).
  - Config: `bitrateMode` constant / variable / quantizer, `latencyMode` quality / realtime, `framerate`, `hardwareAcceleration` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/VideoEncoder/configure)). That `avc.format: 'avc'` yields the avcC description muxers need ⚠️ was not confirmed on MDN.
- **`AudioEncoder` (AAC on Mac Chrome): yes.** `MojoAudioEncoder::IsSupported(kAAC)` follows `kPlatformAudioEncoder`, on by default on Windows, Mac and Android ([mojo_audio_encoder.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/mojo/clients/mojo_audio_encoder.cc), [media_switches.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/base/media_switches.cc)). Limits: 1, 2 or 6 channels; 44100 or 48000 Hz only ([webcodecs/audio_encoder.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/webcodecs/audio_encoder.cc)).
  - The AudioToolbox backend stamps packets from the first input's timestamp, +1024 per packet, so **priming is stamped as signal** ([audio_toolbox_audio_encoder.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/filters/mac/audio_toolbox_audio_encoder.cc)).
  - Measured priming, community figures ⚠️: Chrome on macOS 2112 ([mediabunny #444](https://github.com/Vanilagy/mediabunny/issues/444)), Windows 0, Android 2048 ([#447](https://github.com/Vanilagy/mediabunny/issues/447), Chrome 153). At 44.1 kHz, 2048 = 46.4 ms and 2112 = 47.9 ms, so our ≈ 48 ms doesn't tell them apart.
- **How Apple handles priming.** With no explicit value, playback trims **2112** samples "when starting playback from any point" ([TN2258](https://developer.apple.com/library/mac/technotes/tn2258/_index.html)). An explicit delay needs an `elst` **plus** an `sgpd`/`sbgp` roll group; without that the result is "unspecified" or the 2112 assumption applies ([QTFF](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quicktime-file-format/using_track_structures_to_represent_encode_delay_explictly)).
  - [mediabunny #549](https://github.com/Vanilagy/mediabunny/issues/549) (open) shows the consequence: an elst without a roll group makes AVFoundation cut 2112 **more** (measured there with AVAssetReader).

## §4 Streaming a long take to disk
- **Availability.** `showSaveFilePicker` exists since Chrome 86 ([BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/Window.json)). It needs transient activation, and `keydown` counts except Esc and browser-reserved keys ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/User_activation)). It needs a secure context, and `http://localhost` counts ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Defenses/Secure_Contexts)).
- **`createWritable()`.** Writes go to a temporary file that replaces the target on `close()` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/FileSystemFileHandle/createWritable)); Chrome names it `<name>.crswap` ([file_handle_impl.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/content/browser/file_system_access/file_system_access_file_handle_impl.cc)). Positional writes are supported: `seek`, `write` at a position, `truncate` ([Chrome doc](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/file-system-access)).
- **Close is slow for big files.** At close, `.crswap` ≠ `.mp4` triggers a **SHA-256 over the whole file** plus after-write (Safe Browsing) checks, then quarantine and the move ([safe_move_helper.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/content/browser/file_system_access/file_system_access_safe_move_helper.cc)). Implication: the stop-to-file time grows with size; multi-GB is unmeasured ⚠️.
- **mediabunny targets** ([docs](https://mediabunny.dev/guide/writing-media-files), [formats](https://mediabunny.dev/guide/output-formats)): `StreamTarget(writable)` chunks are positional and "compatible with … `FileSystemWritableFileStream`"; `chunked: true` batches writes into 16 MiB. `BufferTarget` is advised for files under about 100 MB.
  - `fastStart`: `false` puts the moov at the end and uses the least memory; `'fragmented'` is "lightweight to create no matter how large the file becomes"; `'in-memory'` keeps all media in memory.
- **mp4-muxer** ships a `FileSystemWritableFileStreamTarget`.
- **MediaRecorder + File System Access.** Chrome's fMP4 has the moov first and is append-only (S4.3: no trailing `mfra` with a timeslice), so `dataavailable` blobs can be written in order. That does **not** avoid the 4 GiB renderer crash (§1).

## §5 Fixing an existing Chrome MP4
- **ffmpeg behaviour.** The mov demuxer's `use_tfdt` is on by default ("prefer to use the `tfdt` box to set DTS"); `ignore_editlist` is available for comparison ([ffmpeg-formats](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html)). `-itsoffset`: "The offset is added to the timestamps of the input files" ([ffmpeg.html](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html)).
  - The mp4 muxer ([movenc.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavformat/movenc.c)) takes each sample's duration from the gap between consecutive packets (`get_cluster_duration`). It writes `elst` with `media_time = −start_dts` for a negative track start (the mp4 muxer allows negative timestamps, `AVFMT_TS_NEGATIVE`), and writes the `sgpd`/`sbgp` roll group for AAC and Opus.
- **Commands** (⚠️ composed from the facts above, not run on a Chrome take):
  - Durations only: `ffmpeg -i take.mp4 -c copy -movflags +faststart fixed.mp4`
  - Durations + priming (2048 at 44.1 kHz; use `-0.047891` for 2112): `ffmpeg -i take.mp4 -itsoffset -0.046440 -i take.mp4 -map 0:v:0 -map 1:a:0 -c copy -movflags +faststart fixed.mp4`. Expected: an audio `elst` with media_time ≈ 2048 plus a roll group.
  - Check: `ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream=codec_type,start_time,duration fixed.mp4`, then the lead's `av-offset`/`av-avf` scripts.
- **In the browser with mediabunny.** `Conversion` copies packets by default, without re-encoding ([docs](https://mediabunny.dev/guide/converting-media-files)); its demuxer reads `tfdt` and `elst` ([isobmff-demuxer.ts](https://github.com/Vanilagy/mediabunny/blob/main/src/isobmff/isobmff-demuxer.ts)); `BlobSource` reads a File lazily. It has no per-track time-offset option, so an audio shift would go through packet-level sinks and sources ⚠️. In 1.62.1 the resulting elst has no roll group, so AVFoundation double-trims (#549).
- **ffmpeg.wasm.** @ffmpeg/ffmpeg is MIT, 0.12.15 (2025-01-07); @ffmpeg/core is **GPL-2.0-or-later**, 0.12.10 (2025-01-07); the [repo](https://github.com/ffmpegwasm/ffmpeg.wasm) was last pushed 2026-09-29. Handling a multi-GB file in its in-memory FS ⚠️ is unverified.

## §6 Cost and risk of replacing MediaRecorder with WebCodecs
- **API availability.** VideoEncoder, AudioEncoder, VideoFrame and AudioData since Chrome 94; MediaStreamTrackProcessor since 94, marked partial: "Exposed on `Window` instead of `DedicatedWorker`" ([BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/tree/main/api)). All available in Chrome 156.
- **`new VideoFrame(canvas, {timestamp})`** ([video_frame.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/webcodecs/video_frame.cc)). Without `timestamp` it throws a `TypeError`. A texture-backed canvas with GPU compositing is wrapped with `WrapSharedImage`, with **no CPU readback**, unless it has unpremultiplied alpha that isn't discarded. The RGB→YUV step before VideoToolbox was not traced ⚠️.
  - Community numbers: WebGPU canvas → VideoFrame ≈ 1 ms on the GPU; a CPU `copyTo` readback of 1080p 15–20 ms ([tidoust 2022](https://github.com/tidoust/media-tests/blob/main/README.md)).
- **Bitrate and backpressure.** A `framerate: 60` config field reaches VideoToolbox `ExpectedFrameRate` (derived from §2/§3 source). Backpressure: `encodeQueueSize` and the `dequeue` event ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/VideoEncoder)). Every frame must be `close()`d.
- **A/V sync.** Video timestamps are chosen by the app. Audio from a worklet tap needs a clock mapping: `getOutputTimestamp()` returns `contextTime` paired with `performanceTime` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/getOutputTimestamp)). The plan measured the worklet → main message at ≈ 2–3 ms (plan S1.2 R9). mediabunny auto-syncs only MediaStream sources, by first arrival (§3).
- **Ownership moves to the app.** AAC priming and the `elst`/roll group (§3) become our job. In return, the Chromium MP4 muxer, with both its bugs, leaves the path.
- **Unchanged risk.** rAF pauses in a background tab, so recording freezes there too (render §5).
