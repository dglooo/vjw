# Skeptic challenge: GPU particles, fluids, GLTF 3D (design_gpu3d.md, lead provisional pick A) after spike S1-P

## TL;DR
- **BLOCKED: 0 BLOCKER · 6 HIGH · 4 MEDIUM · 1 LOW.** Variant A's frame holds: one context, plain natives, three only for GLTF. Its cost numbers, the R1 argument and two lifecycle details do not hold.
- **R1:** "B cannot lower the cost that decides R1" (L469) is false for a WebGPU compute splat. A compute splat has no per-point raster stage, and raster is ≈ 7.7 ms of the 1 M frame. **Verdict: (a) now + (b) one bounded spike; (c) no spike** (§3).
- **N\* = 131 072 was measured bare** (no HDR target, no bloom, no tone map; 0.33 ms headroom). The arm-0 look adds +2.6 ms p95. Re-derive N\* on the layer as shipped.
- **R3:** the in-repo Serpent bench shows that 4× MSAA adds +2.6 to +3.3 ms at 1728×1118. three r187 hard-codes ≥ 4× MSAA on the transmission target, and glass refracts a white clear colour. Default to 1 sample and replace `transmission`.
- **Lifecycle:** the model layer warms only at Start, for starred scenes, so it stays black everywhere else. Fix: load in `create()` (the milk pattern).

## §2 Checked and holding
- **Cost per visible point** (s1p.md L7) holds: 1 → 2 px adds ≤ 0.5 ms; res 0.5 saves 0.9 ms at 1 M; with all points clipped the frame bills 3.9–4.4 ms (s1p-diag.log, s1p-lever.log).
- **N\* = 131 072 is robust for the bare layer in slow class:** 6/6 rounds have p95 4.49–4.71; 196 608 fails 6/6 rounds (≥ 6.18) (s1p-diag.log). C2 limits this to the bare layer.
- **1 M alone runs at 60 fps:** 0 late / 3602 frames for both arms, max 16.8 ms (s1p-run-a0-1M.log, s1p-run-a1-1M.log).
- **Keep TF:** ping-pong shows no consistent gain (s1p.md L6).
- **B as drawn cannot lower the raster term.** WebGPU point-list primitives are 1 px, so 2 px needs 2 triangles per particle (L338). The claim holds for raster; C1 covers compute.
- **WebGL2 has no scatter path except the rasterizer:** no compute, no storage buffers, no atomics, and TF writes linear buffers. So option (c) has no mechanism (§3).
- **The density rule keeps brightness constant across governor steps.** n ∝ W·H, so per-pixel density and mean luma stay constant. I tested a "brightness pump" hypothesis, and it fails. Luma ∝ N holds only at a fixed size (lumaMed 0.0317 at 1 M against 0.0037 at 131 072).
- **Own depth inside a native works:** Serpent precedent, a DEPTH_COMPONENT24 attachment with DEPTH_TEST reset after use (`scenes/serpent.ts:714`, `:794`).
- **Layer alpha (design gap G1):** `normal` is a straight-alpha over (live.ts:100, :132), so a model can composite over other layers. C7 covers the premultiply mismatch.
- **Lazy chunk:** `import('three')` keeps three out of the main bundle. The engine awaits a def's `warm()` (live.ts:652–653). This holds for starred scenes only; C6 covers the rest.
- **Clocks:** the existing particle natives clamp dt at 0.05 (particles.ts:138, points.ts:229), although `f.dt` itself is unclamped (app.ts:276). Reuse the clamp in the new natives.
- **WebGPU cost is measurable with today's bench.** Our timer reads copy + cross-context wait, which tracks the source's GPU time (research §1, milk median 7.61 ms). Chrome quantizes `timestamp-query` to 100 µs, which is enough for the ms-scale S1-W stage 1.

## §3 Verdict
**BLOCKED.** 0 BLOCKER · 6 HIGH (C1–C6) · 4 MEDIUM · 1 LOW. Each HIGH closes on evidence, not prose: a designer `## Skeptic response`, then a recheck. C4, C5 and C9 close inside S1-T; C1 and C2 close with S1-W and the shipped-layer bench; C3, C6 and C10 close as plan or design text.

**R1 path: (a) now + (b) as one bounded spike; (c) no spike.**
- **(a) Accept N\* + a count knob.** Ship the WebGL2 TF layer.
  - Starred default: N\*_shipped (C2). Expected, computed from s1p-diag and s1p-lever: 65 536–131 072 at res 1 with a look, or 131 072–196 608 at res 0.5.
  - Count knob up to 1 048 576, marked L-class. Measured alone: 11.1–14.7 ms billed, 0 late / 3602.
  - (a) is needed in every outcome: it is the fallback when WebGPU is absent, after `device.lost`, and on the unprobed Windows PC.
- **(b) WebGPU compute-splat spike S1-W (C1).** It is the only path that removes the 7.7 ms raster term.
  - Expected billed p95 at 1 M, res 1, bare = 1.2 fixed + 0.7 sim (arm-1 measured) + 0.6 resolve + 0.7–2.0 copy (design estimate, unmeasured) + A = **3.2–4.5 ms + A**. A is the atomics term, unmeasured on the M4.
  - At res 0.5: ≈ **2.3–2.6 ms + A**.
  - Pass is plausible only if A ≤ 0.5–1.8 ms at res 1, or A ≤ 2.4 ms at res 0.5, before the look is added (C2).
  - Contention is low: in the 1 M still, the disc covers ≈ 3·10⁵ px, so ≈ 3 particles per pixel.
  - Cost: one coder dispatch, ≈ 45–60 min. Stage 1 kills it before any interop work.
- **(c) No WebGL2 spike.** S1-P already measured both levers such a spike would test.
  - A lower-res accumulation target is the res lever: −0.9 ms at 1 M (13.80 against ≈ 14.6, s1p.md L29).
  - A 2-pass density splat still rasterizes 1 M points in pass 1.
  - The non-raster floor at 1 M is ≈ 3.5 ms (1.2 + 0.7 + 1.6, s1p.md L7). Raster would need ≤ 1.5 ms, a 5× cut from the measured 7.7 ms.

### §4.3 Corrections to my round-1 text
- §2, "the density rule keeps brightness constant across governor steps": true only for a fixed-px point (S1-P's `SPIKE.px`). On `scenes/particles.ts` the point px scales with the target height (particles.ts:71, :140), so the rule dims the layer by res² × s². The response quotes this line (L516). That use is withdrawn: see N1.
- §2, "WebGPU cost is measurable with today's bench": this was inferred from the WebGL → WebGL milk copy. For a WebGPU source it stays an assumption until S1-W stage 2 compares billed with timestamp-query: see N4.
- C7, recommended change "write straight alpha": it is complete for `normal` only: see N3.

### §4.4 Checked and holding
- Memory: 4 buffers × vec2 float32 (particles.ts:110–111) = 32 B per point, so 33.5 MB at 1 M. The ⚠️ at L536 holds.
- Texture units that three leaves bound cannot corrupt our uploads. Every upload site binds its texture first: engine.ts:108, milkdrop/native.ts:29, camera/camera.ts:304 and :402, scenes/shatter.ts:747. Rule 2 still applies (L114): three's cache re-syncs before each render.
- L573 says that Start's throwaway `create()` preloads the model. For a def with `warm()`, Start awaits `warm()` instead (live.ts:653). The outcome is the same and the named path is wrong; this is harmless.
- The headless WebGPU precondition of S1-W stage 1 has a precedent: the camera `rvm` arm (ORT-Web WebGPU, headless).
- A chrome or glass miss routes to the lead, and the design waives nothing (L648).
- Decision 6 (a) (extend `scenes/particles.ts`, no second native) and "no WebGPU hooks before S1-W" (L691) are the simplest path.
